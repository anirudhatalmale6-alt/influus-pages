Why creators

The recommendation is the product.

An audience follows a creator for the creator. That is the whole asset, and it is also why a badly matched campaign does more damage than no campaign.

  • It reaches people who opted in, not people who were targeted
  • It is made in a format that audience already watches
  • The content stays usable on your own channels afterwards
  • It can be repeated with the same creator, and gets cheaper

The process

From brief to reporting.

Nothing is contracted before you have seen names.

  1. The brief

    Product, audience, market, budget and what a win looks like. One call is usually enough.

  2. The shortlist

    We come back with named creators, their audience, and why each one fits — not a follower count on its own.

  3. Terms

    Deliverables, dates, exclusivity and usage rights agreed and contracted before anyone films anything.

  4. Production

    Creators produce, you review against the brief, revisions happen before publication.

  5. Reporting

    Reach, engagement and link performance per creator, plus the assets you are licensed to reuse.

The part nobody sees

Sourcing, negotiating, chasing, checking.

Finding the creator is the short part. What takes the time is everything between the yes and the published post.

  • Contracts, exclusivity and usage rights
  • Product logistics and delivery
  • Brief, revisions and sign-off
  • Reporting once it is live

Two sides

What each side gets out of it.

A matchmaking service that only serves the brand runs out of creators. One that only serves creators has nothing to offer them.

For the brand

  • A shortlist of names, with their audience and what they would deliver
  • One contract, one point of contact, one invoice
  • Content you approve before it is published
  • Usage rights written down, not assumed

For the creator

  • Briefs that fit what they actually make
  • Terms agreed before shooting, not after
  • Payment terms in the contract
  • A brand relationship that can be repeated, not a one-off

What gets measured

Four numbers, and the one that is not on the list.

Reach is the easiest number to report and the least useful. These are the ones worth agreeing on before the brief is signed.

Engagement on the post itself

Comments and saves say more than likes. They are also harder to buy.

Audience overlap with your buyer

Country, language and age of the creator’s audience, not of the creator.

What happened on your side

Traffic, sign-ups, orders — measured with a link or a code that belongs to that creator alone.

Whether it can be repeated

A creator who performs once is a result. One who performs twice is a channel.

The number deliberately absent is a guaranteed view count. Nobody can promise one honestly, and a service that does is quoting you a figure it cannot control.

Questions

Before you brief us.

How do you choose the creators?

By fit before size: what the audience is, where it is, how it behaves, and whether the creator has worked with anything adjacent to your category. Follower count sets the tier; it does not pick the creator.

Is there a minimum audience size?

We work from 10K followers upward. Below that the reporting is too noisy to price honestly.

Who owns the content?

The creator does, unless usage rights are bought — so they are negotiated up front and written into the agreement, not asked for afterwards.

What does it cost?

Creator fees are quoted per campaign, because they depend on tier, deliverables, exclusivity and usage rights. You get the shortlist with a fee against each name before you commit.

Can you combine this with press?

Yes, and it is usually the right move: the press placement gives you the credible source, the creators give you the reach that sends people to it.
